Search for a caregiver app and you will find hundreds, many built for professional care agencies rather than families. Most family caregivers do not need a platform that does everything. They need one or two simple tools that the whole family actually opens.

So instead of a ranking, this list is sorted by job: sharing updates, coordinating who does what, keeping track of medicines, and knowing your parent is OK each day. Pick the job that hurts most right now and start there.

A note on honesty: we make I Love My Parents, which is in the last section. We have kept the rest of the list fair.

## How to choose a caregiver app

Before you download anything, answer four questions:

1. **Who will use it?** An app that only you open will not reduce the load. If siblings will not install it, choose something that works by email or a shared link.
2. **Does your parent need to use it?** Some apps are for the family only. Others need your parent to tap or answer. If they do, the app has to be very simple, with large text and no passwords.
3. **What does it cost for the whole family?** Some apps are free for one person and paid per family member.
4. **What happens to the data?** Health details are sensitive. Check who can see what, and whether your parent can see it too.

## Apps for sharing updates with family and friends

### CaringBridge

A free, nonprofit site and app for posting health updates to a wider circle: relatives, friends, neighbours. People can leave messages and offer help.

- **Best for:** a hospital stay or serious diagnosis, when lots of people want news and you cannot answer them all.
- **Not for:** day-to-day coordination between two or three siblings.
- **Price:** free.

## Apps for coordinating who does what

### Caring Village

A care coordination hub with a shared calendar, tasks, medication lists, documents and team chat.

- **Best for:** families who share care between several people and want one place for everything.
- **Not for:** families who only need one function. It can feel like a lot.
- **Price:** free plan, paid plans from about $14.99 a month.

### Lotsa Helping Hands

A free calendar where friends and community members sign up for meals, rides and visits.

- **Best for:** organising help from a wider circle, such as church, neighbours or old colleagues.
- **Price:** free.

### Cozi

Not built for caregiving, but a simple shared family calendar with lists. Many families already use it.

- **Best for:** appointments and shopping lists that the whole family can see.
- **Price:** free tier, paid upgrade available.

## Apps for medicines

### Medisafe

A well-known medication reminder app. It sends reminders, tracks refills, and can alert a family member when a dose is missed.

- **Best for:** a parent who is comfortable with a smartphone and takes several medicines.
- **Price:** free, with a paid premium tier.

### MyTherapy

A free medication reminder app with a health diary for symptoms and measurements such as blood pressure.

- **Best for:** a parent who also tracks readings for the doctor.
- **Price:** free.

If your parent prefers paper, a [free printable medication chart](/blog/free-printable-medication-chart-for-elderly/) on the fridge does the same job without a phone.

## Apps for location and safety

### Life360

A family location sharing app with place alerts, and crash detection on paid plans.

- **Best for:** a parent who still drives or travels alone and agrees to share location.
- **Not for:** most parents at home. Constant location sharing can feel like surveillance, so only use it with real agreement.
- **Price:** free tier, paid safety plans.

## Apps for a daily check-in

### I Love My Parents (our app)

A daily check-in for older parents. Your parent taps one big "I am OK" button once a day. If the tap does not come, the app reminds them first, then tells the family, and keeps telling the family until someone answers. There are also pill reminders grouped by time of day, so you see missed doses the same day.

- **Best for:** an independent parent who lives alone, and family members who want to know they are fine without calling every morning to ask.
- **Not for:** emergencies. It is not an emergency service, and a parent at high risk of falls also needs a medical alert device.
- **Price:** the daily check-in is free forever. The Family plan with pill reminders is $6.99 a month or $59.99 a year.

## Do you need one app or several?

Most families do best with two: one for coordination between the adults (a shared calendar or Caring Village), and one simple tool your parent actually uses (a check-in or medication reminder). Trying to make your parent use a big coordination app usually fails, and it is not what they need.

Whatever you pick, agree on it as a family. One app everyone opens beats three apps that each person half uses. If you live far from your parent, our guide to [long-distance caregiving](/blog/long-distance-caregiving/) shows how these tools fit into a wider plan.
